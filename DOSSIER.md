# Unteralterbach / BerndSoft

## Historical Research Dossier

---

# Introduction

**Unteralterbach** and the **BerndSoft** project emerged from a small but distinctive corner of the German-speaking internet during the early 2010s. What initially appeared to be a relatively obscure game project developed into a much broader historical subject involving pseudonymous developers, artists, programmers, web communities, archived websites, recordings, independent projects, and surviving traces spread across different parts of the early German internet.

This investigation began with a straightforward objective: **to identify the people behind the aliases associated with BerndSoft and reconstruct who actually contributed to Unteralterbach.** As more material was recovered, the investigation expanded beyond the creators themselves. The research came to include the project's development history, associated communities, historical aliases, external contributors, event appearances, archived communications, related media, and surviving material concerning people represented within the project.

The research is based primarily on surviving historical evidence. **Primary project files, archived websites, contemporary records, direct correspondence, and first-hand statements are given greater weight than later recollections or repeated community claims.** Where evidence conflicts, the contradiction is preserved. Where an identification cannot be conclusively established, it is presented as unresolved rather than being converted into a fact through repetition.

The purpose of this dossier is therefore to reconstruct the historical record surrounding **Unteralterbach and BerndSoft**, establish the identities and roles that can be supported by surviving evidence, and document the remaining questions surrounding the project and its contributors.

# 1. Research Objective

The central objective of the investigation is the identification of the people operating behind the pseudonyms associated with **BerndSoft / Unteralterbach**.

The investigation subsequently expanded into several related areas:

* reconstruction of the development history of Unteralterbach;
* identification of BerndSoft contributors;
* reconstruction of historical aliases;
* examination of the project's internal credits and source files;
* recovery of archived websites and media;
* examination of historical German-speaking internet communities;
* investigation of CCC and SIGINT connections;
* examination of contemporary correspondence;
* reconstruction of related characters and real-world source material;
* preservation of conflicting evidence;
* identification of remaining archival leads.

The investigation distinguishes between:

**Confirmed evidence** — directly supported by surviving primary or contemporary material.

**Supported claims** — supported by multiple relevant sources but not equivalent to direct primary proof.

**Research leads** — plausible connections that justify further investigation but are not established.

**Speculation** — claims for which the surviving evidence is insufficient.

# 13. CCC / Jochim Selzer Correspondence

Jochim Selzer was contacted regarding surviving SIGINT12/SIGINT13 records.

His response provides useful information about what CCC could and could not reconstruct.

According to Jochim:

* CCC generally did not maintain records of who attended events;
* ticket purchases were associated with anonymous tokens;
* speakers and members had specific registration procedures;
* ordinary attendees did not register for individual talks;
* there was no system recording who entered a specific talk;
* CCC therefore could not determine who was present in an individual room;
* filming the audience from the stage was prohibited;
* available records included recordings, speaker lists, ticket counts and wristband counts.

Jochim also discussed possible legal retention periods for financial records, but did not expect 13-year-old records to provide useful identification information.

Even if old booking information survived, a booking identifier would not necessarily identify the person who attended.

This effectively rules out reconstructing the SIGINT12 attendee list through ordinary CCC registration records.

---

# 14. SIGINT Photography

Jochim also explained the CCC's approach to photography.

CCC's photography policy requires consent from recognizable people appearing in photographs or video.

# 18. Maja / Dutch Girl Branch

The **Maja** branch concerns a real Dutch girl who was subjected to serious online stalking and harassment.

A contemporary **AD** article from June 2015 independently documents the real-world stalking incident involving a 13-year-old Dutch girl.

Contemporary Unteralterbach-related discussion connects the character Maja to the same Dutch girl and places the relevant events within the same period.

Additional surviving contextual and photographic material supports the connection.

Unlike some of the other identity branches, the evidence available to this investigation is considered sufficient to treat **Maja and the documented Dutch girl as the same person**.

The remaining uncertainty concerns the use of names and identities surrounding the girl after the incident.

An anonymous source contacted during the investigation stated that the people involved later used different names/identities. This is source-reported information and has not been independently established in every detail.

The source also indicated that current online accounts exist, but **those accounts are deliberately not included or pursued here**. The purpose of the investigation is the historical reconstruction of Unteralterbach, not locating the individual in the present day.

The Maja branch is therefore considered **supported and effectively closed** unless genuinely new historical primary evidence emerges.

# 25. Evidence Hierarchy

The investigation uses the following hierarchy when evaluating claims:

### Tier 1 — Primary evidence

* project source files;
* original game files;
* archived websites;
* WARC captures;
* direct contemporary records;
* original correspondence;
* contemporary event programs.

### Tier 2 — Independent contemporary evidence

* historical news reports;
* independent archives;
* contemporary databases;
* historical distribution records;
* contemporaneous external websites.

### Tier 3 — First-hand testimony

* statements from people directly involved;
* direct responses from historical participants;
* contemporaneous personal accounts.

### Tier 4 — Secondary/community evidence

* forum discussions;
* imageboard posts;
* later recollections;
* community reconstructions.

### Tier 5 — Speculation

* username similarities;
* unverified identity claims;
* voice resemblance;
* modern theories;
* repeated claims without independent supporting evidence.

The existence of a claim in a lower tier does not make it useless. It may provide a valuable lead. It simply does not receive the same evidentiary weight as primary material.

**Berlinbernd → Nils Dagsson Moskopp**

# 29. Research Method

The investigation follows a basic principle:

**Reconstruct the history from what survived, rather than reconstructing the evidence around a predetermined theory.**

Primary artifacts are preserved and examined before conclusions are drawn.

Secondary claims are compared against primary evidence.

Contradictions are recorded rather than silently removed.

An identity is not upgraded from a lead to an established finding merely because the same claim appears repeatedly in different communities.

Where an anonymous source provides information, the source's claim is distinguished from independently verified evidence.

Current personal information is excluded when it is unnecessary to the historical objective.

The purpose is to document the historical development of **Unteralterbach and BerndSoft**, the people associated with it, and the surviving evidence surrounding them.

# 30. Conclusion

The surviving evidence demonstrates that Unteralterbach was not an isolated work but the product of a documented pseudonymous development group operating within a wider German-speaking internet environment.

The recovered project files provide the strongest surviving evidence of the team's internal structure. They identify major contributors, document their roles, preserve additional names connected to testing and production, and establish direct historical contact points.

Subsequent archival research has connected the project to wider communities, Debian distribution history, German internet culture, CCC events, SIGINT12, historical forums and independent contemporary records.

At the same time, the investigation demonstrates the limitations of the surviving record. Several real-world identities remain unresolved, and some older community identifications conflict with the project's own primary credits. These contradictions are part of the historical record and should not be concealed in order to produce a cleaner narrative.

The investigation therefore remains open where the evidence remains incomplete.

Future discoveries should be evaluated against the existing primary archive, with particular attention given to surviving project builds, historical forum material, SIGINT recordings, archived communications and other contemporary artifacts.

The objective is ultimately not to force every alias into a confirmed identity, but to establish **what can actually be known about BerndSoft and Unteralterbach from the evidence that has survived.**
